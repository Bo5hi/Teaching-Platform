In short

Use these three worksheets during the lab. Print this page, or copy the tables into a document or spreadsheet. You record your ratings in the questionnaire workbook, not here. Together, the workbook and these worksheets make your Week 9 fieldwork package. A reader should be able to follow every rating back to an interview (INT), an evidence item (EV), or a scan result (SCAN).

Before you start: key words

Open question
A question that needs a full answer. It often starts with "how", "what", "who", or "tell me about". Example: "How do you know when a backup fails?"
Evidence ID
The label of an item from the evidence library, such as EV-04. Write "none" if no evidence was given.
Source ID
The label of any source: an interview (INT-01 to INT-05), an evidence item (EV-01 to EV-21), or a scan result (SCAN-01 to SCAN-03).
Contradiction
A place where what a person said does not match the evidence, or does not match what another person said. When this happens, trust the evidence.
Gap
A place where a control is missing, weak, or not fully working, including where you could not get evidence.

Where to record your ratings

You rate Northgate question by question in the questionnaire workbook: materials/week9/northgate-questionnaire.xlsx. For each question, choose one response level, and write the source IDs that support it.

Response levelScoreChoose this when...
Fully100%The control is fully in place, it works, and the evidence proves it.
Almost75%The control is in place and works, with only small gaps.
Mostly50%About half is in place. Important parts are missing, out of date, or not working everywhere.
Somewhat25%Only a small or informal part exists, for example a draft, an old document, or "it's in my head".
Not Compliant0%The control is missing, or there is no evidence that it exists.

A rating without evidence is only an opinion. If a person says something is done but gives no evidence, the rating is usually Somewhat or Not Compliant, and the missing evidence goes on your gaps list.

Tips for a good interview

  • Start with an easy question, such as "Tell me about your role and what a normal week looks like."
  • Ask "how" and "show me". A vague question gets a vague answer.
  • After each answer, ask for proof: "Is that written down?" or "Can you share a report that shows that?"
  • Ask follow-up questions about numbers and dates: "How many?", "When was the last time?", "Who else can do that?"
  • Be kind and do not blame. James is one person doing a big job.
  • Listen for confident words such as "everyone", "always", and "the same day". Ask for proof of those answers first.
  • Before you finish, read your notes back to check you understood correctly.

What to do with this

  1. Complete Worksheet 1 during your interviews in the interview simulator (INT-01 to INT-05).
  2. Compare what people told you with the evidence. Log every disagreement in Worksheet 2.
  3. Rate each question in the questionnaire workbook with one of the five response levels, and cite source IDs for every rating.
  4. Complete Worksheet 3, including controls with no evidence.
  5. Check that every rating can be traced to an interview, an evidence ID, or a scan result.
