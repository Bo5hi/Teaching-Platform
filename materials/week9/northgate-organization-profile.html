Client Brief: Northgate Supply Co. NIST CSF 2.0 Assessment

Your engagement letter, the organization profile, and the rules for fieldwork.

Fictional scenario for training. All names, companies, and addresses are made up.

In short

Northgate Supply Co. is a small wholesale company. In April 2026 an attacker broke into its file server and stole finance files. Now the CEO has hired your team to check Northgate's security against the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0. This week you do the fieldwork: you interview five Northgate staff members, review Northgate's documents and the MCCoE scan results, and score a 41-question assessment questionnaire. Next week (Week 10) you turn your fieldwork into a report and present it to Northgate's leaders.

1. Engagement letter

From: Linda Harper <lharper@northgate-supply.example>
To: MCCoE Assessment Team
Date: Tuesday, 1 September 2026
Subject: Engagement: NIST CSF 2.0 security assessment of Northgate Supply Co.

Dear MCCoE Assessment Team,

Thank you for agreeing to assess Northgate Supply Co. This letter confirms what we are asking you to do.

Why now. In April 2026 we had a security incident. An attacker used an employee's stolen password to get into our file server and took files from our finance share. We contained it the same morning, but it showed us that we do not really know how strong our security is. Our cyber insurance renews in November, and the insurer is asking what we have changed.

Scope. Please assess Northgate's cybersecurity program against the six functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. This covers our office, warehouse, servers, cloud email, and the vendors who connect to our systems.

Testing. We give the MCCoE team written permission to run one round of network and vulnerability scans (an external scan from the internet, an internal network scan, and a vulnerability scan), at a time agreed with James. Please do not log in to or change any of our systems, and do not try to break in. The rest of your work is interviews and a review of documents and settings that we give you.

Fieldwork dates. 8 to 12 September 2026. Report due: 25 September 2026. Outbrief (a meeting where you present your results to me and my managers): after we receive the report.

Point of contact. James Chen, our IT Manager, will be your main contact. He can answer your questions and share documents and reports. I, Alicia Brown (HR), Helen Ortiz (Finance), and Tunde Okafor (Warehouse) will also meet your team. You can reach James at jchen@northgate-supply.example or 555-0147. If you need me, call 555-0142.

What I want at the end. An honest picture. For each area, tell me what is working, what is missing, and what we should fix first. I would rather hear bad news from you than from the next attacker.

Confidentiality. Please treat everything you learn as confidential and use it only for this assessment.

Regards,
Linda Harper
Chief Executive Officer, Northgate Supply Co.

5. What happened in April 2026

This is a plain-language summary. You studied this incident in Week 7.

  1. In early April, Maria Rivera got a phishing email (a fake email that tries to trick you). She typed her password into a fake login page. Her account did not have MFA.
  2. At 2:14 a.m. on Wednesday 15 April, the attacker used her password to log in to the file server ng-files01. This worked because an old firewall setting (a port-forward) let people reach the server's SSH remote login from the internet, even though the rules said remote access must go through the VPN.
  3. The attacker gave themselves full admin rights through a badly configured setting, created a hidden account, and set up a scheduled task so they could come back later.
  4. They copied the finance folder, including vendor bank details, to a computer on the internet.
  5. James Chen saw unusual outbound traffic the next morning. He blocked the attacker, removed the hidden account, and closed the old port-forward the same day.

Northgate made several changes after the incident. Part of your job is to check which changes really happened and how well they work.

6. What leadership is worried about

  • "Could it happen again?" Linda wants to know if the same kind of attack would still work today.
  • Cyber insurance. The policy renews in November 2026. The insurer has asked about MFA, tested backups, and an incident response plan.
  • Customer and vendor trust. Vendors were notified that their bank details were exposed. Leadership does not want a second letter.
  • One-person IT. James does almost everything. What happens when he is sick or on vacation?
  • Limited budget. Northgate is a small company. Leadership needs to know what to fix first.

7. Rules for fieldwork

  • Use only the evidence provided: the Northgate evidence library, your interview transcripts and notes, and the MCCoE scan results. Do not invent evidence, and do not guess what a document "probably" says.
  • Never use real data. Do not add real company information, real screenshots, real passwords, or anything about a real employer to your fieldwork package.
  • You do not scan or log in to anything yourself. The MCCoE team already ran the scans that Northgate approved in writing. You review their results.
  • Be respectful. James is helping you. Ask questions without blaming anyone. People share more when they do not feel judged.
  • Treat your notes as confidential client information. Share them only with your team and your instructor.

8. How fieldwork works here

MCCoE assessments follow the same method every time. You answer a questionnaire, you back up every answer with evidence, and you write down where the evidence came from.

Step 1. Score the questionnaire

Open the questionnaire workbook, northgate-questionnaire.xlsx. It has 41 questions. Each question is linked to a NIST CSF 2.0 category (for example GV.PO, policy) and has a weight: a number that shows how important the question is. For each question, choose one of five levels:

LevelScoreWhat it means
Fully100%The practice is in place, written down where needed, and working everywhere. The evidence proves it.
Almost75%The practice is in place and working, with only small gaps.
Mostly50%The practice is partly in place. There are real gaps, or it covers only some people or systems.
Somewhat25%The practice is informal, just started, or has very little evidence.
Not Compliant0%The practice is not in place, or there is no evidence for it at all.

The workbook multiplies each level by the question's weight. It adds these up for each CSF function and for Northgate as a whole, so heavier questions count more.

Step 2. Gather evidence from three sources

  1. Policy and artifact review. Documents, exports, and reports from Northgate, such as policies, settings, and logs. Each has an ID (EV-01, EV-02, and so on) in the evidence library. Open an item only when an interview gives you its ID.
  2. Interviews. Interview all five staff members in the interview simulator (INT-01 to INT-05). Each person has limited time, so plan first. Before the interviews, write at least five open questions (questions that need a full answer, not just yes or no) that cover different CSF functions. Example: "How does a new employee get access to the file server?" is better than "Do you control access?" After each answer, ask for proof: "Can you show me that?" or "Is that written down anywhere?"
  3. Scans. The MCCoE team ran network and vulnerability scans with Northgate's written permission. Read the scan results (SCAN-01, SCAN-02, SCAN-03). Scans show what is really on the network, which is not always what the documents say.

Step 3. Record the evidence for every answer

  • Next to every answer in the workbook, list the IDs of the evidence you used, for example "EV-04, INT-02, SCAN-01". If you can, add the row, line, or section.
  • No evidence is a result too. Sometimes a person says "we don't have that written down" or "I just know how it works." There is no file for that. Write "no evidence provided". That usually means a low score.
  • Compare sources. What people say and what the evidence shows are not always the same. When they disagree, trust the evidence, score on the evidence, and write down the difference.

Use the fieldwork worksheets for your interview notes and your contradictions log.

What to do with this

  1. Lab step 1: read this brief before you write any questions.
  2. Lab step 2: use sections 4 to 6 to plan at least five open questions for James across Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.
  3. Lab steps 3 to 7: follow section 8 while you gather evidence and score the questionnaire.
